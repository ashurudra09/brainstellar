## Question
Contrast TCP and UDP, and give an example application that should use each.

## Hint
The core trade-off is reliability and ordering guarantees versus latency and overhead -- everything else follows from that.

## Answer
TCP is connection-oriented, reliable, and ordered (good for file transfer, HTTP); UDP is connectionless, best-effort, and unordered but much lower overhead (good for live video/voice, DNS).

## Solution
TCP establishes a connection (three-way handshake), guarantees delivery via acknowledgments and retransmission, and preserves byte-stream ordering -- at the cost of handshake latency, per-packet overhead, and head-of-line blocking (a lost packet stalls everything behind it until it's retransmitted). That makes it the right choice whenever correctness of the data matters more than the delay of a retransmit: HTTP, file transfer, database connections.

UDP sends packets with no connection setup, no delivery guarantee, and no ordering -- a lost or out-of-order packet is simply gone or ignored by the application layer. That's exactly right for real-time media (a dropped video frame from a second ago is worthless anyway; better to skip it and move on) and for short request/response protocols like DNS, where the cost of an occasional retry is lower than the cost of TCP's connection overhead on every single query.
