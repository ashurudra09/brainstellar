## Question
Variant A converts 5.0% of 10,000 visitors; variant B converts 5.4% of 10,000 visitors. Is this difference statistically significant at the 5% level, and what would you tell the team?

## Hint
This is a two-proportion test -- compute the pooled standard error under the null hypothesis that both variants have the same true conversion rate, then see how many standard errors apart the observed rates are.

## Approach
Pooled proportion: $\hat{p} = (500 + 540) / 20000 = 0.052$. Standard error: $SE = \sqrt{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})(1/n_A + 1/n_B)} = \sqrt{0.052 \times 0.948 \times 2/10000} \approx 0.00314$. $z = (0.054 - 0.05) / 0.00314 \approx 1.27$.

## Answer
$z \approx 1.27$, which is well below the 1.96 threshold for a 5% two-sided test -- not statistically significant.

## Solution
A $z$-score of 1.27 corresponds to a two-sided p-value of roughly 0.20, far above the conventional 0.05 cutoff, so we fail to reject the null hypothesis that the two variants have the same true conversion rate. The 0.4 percentage-point observed gap is plausible under pure sampling noise at this sample size.

What to tell the team: this test is underpowered to detect a 0.4pp lift at these rates -- don't ship B based on this result, and don't conclude A and B are equivalent either. If a 0.4pp lift is worth detecting, run a power calculation for the required sample size before extending the test, rather than eyeballing whether the raw percentages "look" different.
