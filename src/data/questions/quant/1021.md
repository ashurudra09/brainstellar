## Question
Nine children of distinct ages (a whole number of years each) were born at a fixed interval of time between consecutive children. The sum of the squares of the ages of all nine children equals the square of their father's age. Given that the father is aged at most 60, how old is the father?

## Hint
Don't parametrise by the youngest child — use the *middle* child's age. The cross terms then cancel when you square and add. Once you have the resulting equation, look at it modulo 3.

## Answer
48

## Solution
### Setting up

Let $a$ be the age of the **middle (5th)** child and $d$ the common gap, so the nine ages are $a-4d,\ a-3d,\ \dots,\ a+4d$. Centering on the middle child makes the $\pm 2ajd$ cross terms cancel in pairs when we square and sum:

$$\sum_{j=-4}^{4}(a+jd)^2 = 9a^2 + 2d^2(1^2+2^2+3^2+4^2) = 9a^2 + 60d^2$$

So with $F$ the father's age,

$$9a^2 + 60d^2 = F^2$$

Since the ages are whole numbers, $a$ and $d$ are positive integers.

### Descent modulo 3

The left side is divisible by $3$, so $3 \mid F^2$, hence $3 \mid F$. Write $F = 3k$:

$$9k^2 = 9a^2 + 60d^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3k^2 = 3a^2 + 20d^2$$

Now $3$ divides $3k^2 - 3a^2 = 20d^2$, and $\gcd(3,20) = 1$, so $3 \mid d$. Write $d = 3e$:

$$3k^2 = 3a^2 + 180e^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad k^2 - a^2 = 60e^2$$

### Difference of squares

$$(k-a)(k+a) = 60e^2$$

The two factors have the same parity and their product is even, so both are even: put $k - a = 2m$ and $k + a = 2n$. Then $4mn = 60e^2$, i.e.

$$mn = 15e^2, \qquad a = n-m, \quad k = n+m, \quad F = 3(n+m)$$

### Applying the constraints

Two conditions filter the candidates: $F \le 60$, which forces $n + m \le 20$, and the youngest child must have a positive age, i.e. $a > 4d = 12e$.

**$e = 1$** (so $d = 3$), $mn = 15$:

| $(m,n)$ | $a = n-m$ | $F = 3(n+m)$ | $a > 12$? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $(1,15)$ | $14$ | $48$ | yes |
| $(3,5)$ | $2$ | $24$ | no — the youngest would be $2 - 12 < 0$ |

**$e = 2$** (so $d = 6$), $mn = 60$, needing $a > 24$ and $n + m \le 20$. The admissible-sized pairs $(4,15), (5,12), (6,10)$ give $a = 11, 7, 4$, all far below $24$; the pairs with large enough $a$, such as $(1,60)$, give $F = 183 > 60$. Nothing survives.

**$e \ge 3$**: then $d \ge 9$, so $a > 36$, forcing $k > 36$ and $F > 108$. Impossible.

### Conclusion

The only solution is $F = 48$, with $a = 14$ and $d = 3$: the children are aged

$$2,\ 5,\ 8,\ 11,\ 14,\ 17,\ 20,\ 23,\ 26$$

Check: $9(14^2) + 60(3^2) = 1764 + 540 = 2304 = 48^2$. 

The two moves that do all the work are centering on the middle child to kill the cross terms, and the mod-3 descent which forces both $3 \mid F$ and $3 \mid d$ — that shrinks the search to a handful of factor pairs.
