## Question
At the end of day 0, six magical seeds are planted. On each day following, each seed still remaining a seed has a $1/2$ chance of transforming into an apple tree (independently across seeds). Once a seed becomes a tree, it stays a tree forever. What is the expected number of days until all six seeds have become apple trees?

## Hint
Let $X_i$ be the day seed $i$ sprouts — each $X_i$ is i.i.d. Geometric(1/2) on $\{1,2,3,\dots\}$. You want $E[\max(X_1,\dots,X_6)]$. For a non-negative integer random variable $T$, $E[T]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P(T>n)$, and $P(\max > n) = 1-P(\text{all }X_i\le n)$.

## Answer
$$E[T] = \frac{7880}{1953} \approx 4.035 \text{ days}$$

## Solution
Let $T=\max(X_1,\dots,X_6)$ where each $X_i$ is i.i.d. Geometric(1/2): $P(X_i\le n) = 1-(1/2)^n$ for $n\ge 0$.

### 1. Tail-sum formula
$$E[T]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P(T>n)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[1-\big(1-(1/2)^n\big)^6\Big]$$

### 2. Expand with the binomial theorem
Let $x=1/2$. Expanding $(1-x^n)^6$ and rearranging:
$$1-(1-x^n)^6=\sum_{k=1}^{6}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{6}{k}x^{nk}$$

Summing over $n=0,1,2,\dots$ term-by-term (each is a geometric series in $x^k$, which converges since $x=1/2$):
$$E[T]=\sum_{k=1}^{6}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{6}{k}\frac{1}{1-x^k}=\sum_{k=1}^{6}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{6}{k}\frac{2^k}{2^k-1}$$

### 3. Evaluate each term

| $k$ | $\binom{6}{k}$ | $\dfrac{2^k}{2^k-1}$ | term |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 2 | $+12$ |
| 2 | 15 | 4/3 | $-20$ |
| 3 | 20 | 8/7 | $+160/7$ |
| 4 | 15 | 16/15 | $-16$ |
| 5 | 6 | 32/31 | $+192/31$ |
| 6 | 1 | 64/63 | $-64/63$ |

Summing exactly gives
$$E[T] = \frac{7880}{1953} \approx 4.035$$

### 4. Sanity checks
- With 1 seed, the formula collapses to $E[T] = 2^1/(2^1-1) = 2$, matching the mean of a single Geometric(1/2) variable.
- With 2 seeds, it gives $E[T] = 2\cdot 2 - 1\cdot 4/3 = 8/3 \approx 2.67$, which matches direct numerical summation of $\sum_n \big[1-(1-x^n)^2\big]$.
